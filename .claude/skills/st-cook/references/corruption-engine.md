# Corruption engine — craft guidance for the concept sheet

Read this before Phase 1 of `/st-cook`. It is the distilled, story-agnostic version of what the last full campaign taught: what makes a slow-corruption RP grip, what makes it collapse, and which knobs the concept sheet has to set. Instance detail (names, the creature's biology, the city) belongs in the campaign's gitignored bible, never here.

## 1. The player is the reader — design for the play gap

In prose, dramatic irony runs on a knowledge gap between reader and protagonist. In RP the player *is* the reader and knows everything the protagonist doesn't, so that gap is gone. Rebuild it as a **play gap**: the protagonist makes choices under a false model of what is happening to her, and *those choices are the player's turn content*. She thinks she is doing X; it causes Y. The player writes X and the excuse; the narrator writes Y.

Consequence for the setup: every device in the card, lorebook and Direction exists to keep that split alive. When the narrator starts writing her choices, the player has nothing left to do and sends empty turns — that is the failure mode the whole apparatus guards against.

## 2. The engine — slow self-blame (the "Breaking Bad" shape)

The reader knows it is the creature. The protagonist concludes it is her nature. She is never oblivious to the symptoms — only to the cause — and the first suspect is always herself.

- **The creature never argues.** It creates needs; her own standards do the pushing ("a good wife would…", "a competent professional would…"). Each push is small enough that refusing it would look absurd: a form rounded down, a door already open, a favour, a thank-you.
- **Resistance is mandatory and fails sideways.** She must fight — scrubbing, a clinic visit, a lock, abstinence, a confession attempted — and each attempt produces *evidence against her* rather than a punishment: a worse craving, a blackout, a kinder victim. The player writes the resistance and the verdict she passes on herself; the narrator writes the sideways failure.
- **One belief destroyed per chapter.** Define on page one the two or three things she is proud of being (clean, faithful, kind, professional, human, "not like *them*"). Each chapter takes one. The ending is her accepting she is all of what she hated most — and signing it.
- **Mind-break is identity erosion, slow.** Pleasure is present but never the argument. The argument runs shame → secrecy → complicity → "I chose this" → "I am this". No switch, no sudden break: she stops fighting because fighting would mean admitting she was ever fighting.
- **Monotonic on two axes; no chapter may score lower than the last.** *Radius of harm*: herself → one willing person → one trusting person, deliberately → her household, for weeks, under a lie → strangers downstream → the institution and her successor. *Culpability*: blackout → awake → planned → institutional (she signs it) → inherited (she teaches it, unasked). Write the self-verdict ladder for the concept sheet as five short lines in her voice; it doubles as the chapter spine.
- **Escalation is a change in cost, never in volume.** Two scenes close together with more bodies or more anatomy deflate; a scene that changes what the act *means* to her does not. Every chapter's set-piece needs a new meaning axis.

## 3. Six structural rules (they override any prose the setup generates)

1. **Voice fence.** The narrator writes what happens TO her and AROUND her — bodies, arrivals, other characters, consequences. Every sentence in which she speaks, decides, files, rationalizes or passes a verdict on herself belongs to the player. Where a beat needs one, the narrator holds the moment open and stops.
2. **Menu, not beat sheet.** Each chapter has ONE obligatory arrival (narrator-owned) plus candidate beats; any two or three reach the destination. Unreached beats are not owed; a chapter reached through a beat the player invented has succeeded. A Direction entry is context, not a command — the moment it lists beats "in order" the narrator becomes a script executor.
3. **Decline costs shape, not nothing.** No cure — but the creature never re-takes a decision the player made while lucid. It may take hours she doesn't remember. A refusal buys a slower, uglier, differently shaped chapter, never the same one 48 hours late.
4. **Guards are addressed.** N-GUARD = the narrator may not initiate; if the player forces it, yes-and, don't block, don't punish. H-LIMIT = refuse regardless of source (the player's standing hard limits plus any campaign-specific ones, e.g. a reveal, a cure, the creature speaking).
5. **The mirror is a posture, not a building.** The closing image repeats the opening image's *posture* wherever the playthrough actually left her.
6. **Forks pose situations, not menus.** The message that raises a fork ends on the pressure and never on an implied answer. Consequences live in a separate after-she-chooses block.

## 4. Tempo and turn mechanics

- **Doujinshi tempo.** Open each chapter *inside* the situation (no commute-and-weather preamble); one set-piece within the first third; domestic filler only as the frame around a set-piece; no quiet chapter. Push + resistance + sideways failure all happen inside the set-piece chapter. ~15–20 messages per chapter.
- **Every narrator turn ends on a door** — a person, object, place or rule left unused for the player to pick up. The player's longest turns are always the ones where he adds something the narrator didn't offer, so the door is the engagement mechanism, not decoration.
- **Empty player turn → time and consequence only**, no new contact, no new escalation, still a door. (ST sends *no* user message on an empty send, so phrase this as "if the last message is your own".)
- **Division of labour.** Narrator owns graphic anatomy (her register stops at a few named words), the creature's will, arrivals, consequences, continuity, other characters' interiority. Player owns her decisions, cover stories, self-verdicts, resistance, time cuts, and any new person or place he brings in.
- **Openers carry all the risk.** Almost every swipe in the history happened on the opener and ~none afterwards. Write three tonally distinct openers per chapter: quiet, domestic, exactly one wrong detail, ending on the hold.

## 5. Sizing the campaign

| Shape | When | What compresses |
|---|---|---|
| **One-shot** (1 chapter, 15–25 msgs) | the idea is one situation, one belief, one arrival | the ladder collapses to a single push → resistance → sideways failure → verdict; the closing image is the opening image an hour later |
| **Short** (3 chapters) | one protagonist, one creature/antagonist, one household | beliefs: two proud things + "ever anything else"; radius stops at the household or one stranger |
| **Full** (5 chapters) | an institution or a city is in play | the full monotonic ladder; midpoint = the point of no return (a choice she can't un-make), chapters 4–5 widen the radius past people she knows |

Pick the smallest shape the idea can carry. A scenario is played roughly once; a 5-chapter bible for a one-situation idea is effort that never gets played.

## 6. Axis catalogue (choose 2–4, each chapter opens one)

space (private → shared → public) · time (an hour → weeks → a season) · knowledge (nobody knows → one witness → an accomplice → an institution) · body (one new capability per chapter, cumulative, pleasant to her) · species (a non-human vector — offered, never assumed) · household (the marriage or family as supply line) · institution (her office, stamp, credentials become tools) · inheritance (she induces the next one, having never been inducted herself).

Every named intimate partner is a woman; men are anonymous instruments — this is a standing taste fact, not a per-campaign choice. Communal/group beats are *received, not driven*: offer one door, never add bodies to escalate.

## 7. Ending shapes

- **Terminal + open world**: her arc closes (nothing hidden, nothing rationalized, nothing wanted; the thing she wanted granted in the worst way); the world's arc opens (camera pulls back to an inventory of only the vectors the player actually caused).
- **Inheritance**: she does to a newcomer what was never done to her — an induction she invents.
- **Mirror posture**: the last image is the first image with the woman inside it changed. The last authored sentence is the player's.

Closure test for the concept: after the last chapter, the Direction entry could be deleted with nothing left to steer toward and no mechanic in the lorebook left unplayed.

## 8. What the concept sheet must set (checklist)

premise (one line) · protagonist + wound (what she is *for*; what she is proud of; what she hates most) · antagonist/creature (what it needs, what it never does, the anatomy canon) · engine (the belief ladder + self-verdict ladder) · axes (2–4) · chapter count · opening image (situation, one wrong detail, who is *not* coming) · ending shape · H-LIMIT list · N-GUARD list for chapter 1 · cover words she will reach for in chapter 1 · her dissociation register (blackout / lucid-but-filing / awake).
